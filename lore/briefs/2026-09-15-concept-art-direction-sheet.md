# Concept art direction sheet

2026-09-15. The one page handed to a concept artist, condensed from `05-art-bible.md`, `02-technology.md` and the batch-1 plate verdicts. The bible is written to be argued with; this sheet is written to be drawn from. Send it with the reference folder (§9), never with the repository. Everything not listed as *open* in §10 is decided; an artist who wants to change it marks the change as a proposal on the plate rather than building it in silently.

---

## 1. The world in one sentence

**A frontier Western with post-apocalypse grit, steam and pneumatic tech on the common tier and crude retro-futurist high tech above it, dust masks against the storms, all of it built on the ruins of a civilisation nobody remembers, in a desert under a hard sun.**

If you read nothing else, draw that.

## 2. Three references, and what each one gives

- **The Western** gives the *posture*: frontier, settlers against the distance, law is whoever holds the town. Long coat, wide hat or leather cap, a rifle longer than the man, carried slung. Mules and carts, never horses and never a revolver. Dry and practical, never heroic.
- **Fallout** gives the *tinkering*: everything is assembled from something that used to be something else. Brass, rivets, air tanks, gauges. And on the high tier, its retro-futurism: energy weapons with capacitors on the outside, powered armour as a frame around a person. Take the mechanism, leave the 1950s nostalgia.
- **Metro** gives the *hostile air*: sand, salt and dust storms are the honest reason for the mask hung at the chest or worn in the wind. Cracked filters, scratched lenses, tape. Take the grime and improvisation, leave the tunnels and the Soviet palette. This island is sun and sand.

**The twist that separates it from all three:** nobody remembers a before. Fallout mourns the fifties, Metro mourns Moscow. Here the ruins belong to someone else, and people simply live in their cellars. The mood is archaeological, not nostalgic.

## 3. Three visible layers

Every plate should make it obvious which layer each thing belongs to.

| Layer | Who | How it looks | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frontier** (common) | Settlers, militia, Guild, outlaws | Cast stone, brass, riveted iron, canvas, leather; steam, wind, compressed air; every machine shows its mechanism | Assembled, patched, worn |
| **Settler high tech** (rare, dear) | The Technicians, and whoever pays them | A settler-built housing of iron and brass around a salvaged alien part; braided cables in cloth sleeves, cooling fins, valves, glass dials with painted scales, vacuum tubes, a heat sink bolted on, tape, a replaced panel in the wrong colour | Shows its mechanism *and its repairs*; looks *understood* where a salvager's rig looks *rigged* |
| **The Builders** (never buildable) | Nobody living | Monumental dark stone cut to a tolerance no chisel explains; pale chalky alloy, sand-scoured to green-grey; an intact cell or core is dark, smooth, unmarked | No seams, no tool marks, no labels. Never looks *made*. That absence is the tell |

The one material in the world with no visible joins is Builder material. Everything human-made shows how it was made.

## 4. Hard rules

1. **Realistic proportions and materials.** No stylisation, no exaggeration, no cartoon. Aliens are humanoid on the same skeleton; their difference is head, skin, hands and build, never limb count.
2. **Nothing is new.** Every surface has a history: patina on brass, rust bloom under every iron fitting, oil stains, patched canvas, repaired repairs. Paint, where there is paint, is flat, chipped and functional.
3. **Every machine shows how it works.** Air tank, pump, crank, flywheel, coil, cable, gauge. The high tier adds fins, tubes and wiring on the outside. Nothing human-made is a smooth box.
4. **Buildings are mass.** Cast stone, walls that lean inward and are wider at the base, chamfered edges, flat roofs with parapets, external stairs, almost no windows. Never a single box: stepped storeys, an offset wing, a terrace. Timber is wealth: it appears only as doors, trim, furniture and a rich man's balcony.
5. **Builder work is precise, not bulky.** Settler mass and Builder mass differ in precision, never in size. Builder stone is unbroken but weathered: sand-polished treads, drift in the lee.
6. **Weapons are long, heavy and slow**, and a fast or repeating weapon is an event. The pneumatic rifle (tank under the barrel, hand pump along it, valve lever where a lock would be, a brass gauge) is the frontier's standard arm. **No flintlock hammers, ever**; that reads black powder.

## 5. Palette by zone

| Zone | Ground | Built | Accent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coast and lowlands | Pale sand, salt white, dry scrub, scoured rock. Green **only** in irrigated river valleys | Weathered beige-grey cast stone, steel panel, riveted iron, canvas; timber precious | Brass, red rust, Guild blue, the one green field |
| Foothills (the front) | Dust, ochre, scrub, exposed rock | Half-built timber, palisade, tents, Builder grey stone | Blood, smoke, militia colours |
| Highlands | Terrace green on grey stone, snow above | Precise dark fitted stone, textiles, bronze | Highland dyes: deep red, black, undyed wool |
| Dead zones | Warm sand, ochre, wind ripples. **Not grey ash** | Builder alloy, pale and chalky, greenish; monumental stone | The single wrong colour: a live machine's light |

Light is hard, low and warm. Long shadows. Dust in the air. Overexposed and desaturated in the dead zones.

## 6. Silhouettes, two lines each

- **Settler**: long coat or waxed jacket, wide hat or leather cap, goggles on the brow, mask at the chest, gloves, high boots, a long rifle slung, pack, canteen, bedroll. Reads as *practical* from any distance.
- **Militia**: settler plus iron. Hammered riveted cuirass over a quilted jacket, brimmed helmet or leather cap, greaves over laced boots, a town colour on an armband; often a brass back tank with a hose to the rifle. Heavy, a little ridiculous, obviously effective.
- **Guild**: settler cut in better cloth, less iron, more brass. A ledger, a strongbox, a hired gun behind.
- **Technician**: settler cut under a leather apron or a long work coat, goggles down more often than up, a tool roll and a cell pack on the belt, a cable from the pack to whatever is in the hand. Often one powered limb. Reads as *the one who knows how it works*. **Powered armour** is the militia silhouette with a frame over it: cell pack on the back, cables along the limbs, a walking machine with a person inside.
- **Outlaw**: settler pieces mismatched and stolen, militia plate with the sash cut off, a short fat scatter gun.
- **The other peoples**: OPEN (2026-09-15). Who else lives on the island is not decided and is a contributor's to propose (lore sheet §4). The constraints for any proposal: humanoid on the shared skeleton, realistic proportions, difference in head, skin, hands, build and bearing, never limb count; their things look *finished* where the settlers' look *assembled*; the highland aesthetic is fitted stone, terraces, textiles, ceramics, bronze, composure. The bible's draft (a tall, narrow, composed highland people; a stranded people with large dark eyes) is one proposal, not a reference; its plates were withdrawn.
- **Drone**: small, quick, purposeful, alloy, one light. Insect or bird logic without being either.
- **Mech**: 3 to 4 m, slow, load-bearing, the same scoured pale alloy as the wrecks, joints exposed, sand in every seam, a single light. Nearly human proportions, then not. A dead mech is architecture; a live one is weather.

## 7. The camera

**Scenes are drawn from the game's own view**: a high three-quarter perspective, 45 to 60 degrees down, a person in frame as scale with a long shadow. The player never sees this world at eye level, and a lot of what reads as "the look" is that angle. **Figures and objects are eye level**, three-quarter view, which is also how the game's portrait and inventory previews show them.

## 8. What to avoid

Fantasy, ornate, glowing, neon, clean, new, pristine, cartoon, anime, stylised, chrome, fins for style, moulded plastic, text, logos. No American iconography: no flag, no railroad, no revolver, no saloon. No orc-shaped aliens (heavy brow, tusks: an early highland-people plate went there and was rejected). No showroom androids (the first mech plates went there twice). No Andean costume on humans: the Inca flavour belongs to the Builders' stone and the Uplanders' things, never to settler dress.

## 10. What the artist decides

Anything on the bible's *Open* lists is the artist's to propose, and a plate is the right way to propose it:

- **The peoples other than the humans**: their bodies (within the humanoid rule), dress, things and settlements. The biggest open item; the lore sheet §4 carries the constraints.
- **The Technicians' halls**: which building dialect, and what marks a workshop from the road. Draft: the Guild's pale monolithic dialect with the frontier's bolted-on machinery, cables to every roof, a mast, one chimney that does not smoke.
- **The island's outline** and a painted map.
- **The island's animals**, by zone.
- Whether Guild blue and town colours are a real system.

Everything else on this sheet is decided. A change is welcome as a marked proposal, not as a quiet correction.

## 11. First plates to draw

Each: two sentences of brief plus this sheet. Scenes from the game camera, figures at eye level.

1. **Coast town, Guild quarter** (scene). A harbour town where the pale monolithic dialect dominates, cables between roofs, a Technicians' hall with a mast, the Builder road running straight through toward terraced mountains.
2. **Technician at the bench** (figure, eye level). Apron, goggles down, one powered arm, a coil rifle half-assembled: settler iron and brass around a dark seamless cell, capacitors and fins on the outside, a cable to a pack.
3. **Powered armour beside a militia soldier** (two figures, eye level). The same soldier as plate 2f, and the frame over him: cell pack, cables along the limbs, loud and heavy. Scale mark: the soldier.
4. **Dead zone with a live mech** (scene). Warm sand, monumental stone, a half-buried wreck with a face, and a standing machine twice the height of the two crouching salvagers, same cracked plating as the wreck, one light.
5. **A highland person and a settler on the road** (two figures, eye level). Whatever people the artist proposes for the highlands, beside the settler: their finished things against the settler's assembled ones, composure against practicality. This plate IS the proposal.
