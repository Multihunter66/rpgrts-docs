# Setting and history

Draft 1, 2026-09-04. Placeholder names in `[brackets]`.

## The short version

An island, somewhere. Long ago a civilisation built cities in its highlands and roads across all of it, and then stopped. Nobody alive knows who they were, why they ended, or whether the people living here now are their descendants or strangers who washed up on the ruins. A few generations back, humans arrived and do not remember how. They settled the coast, built towns of timber and riveted iron, and now they are running out of coast. Uphill lives an older people who never left. In the ruins, the old machines are still working.

The game is set at the point where the coast is full and the powers on the island have begun to press on each other. It does not tell the player which side to take, or whether to take one.

## Four layers of time

The history has four layers, and the further back, the less anyone knows. The game never gives the player a clean account of anything older than the founding of the towns.

### 1. The Builders

The civilisation that fell. Everything about them is inference from what they left:

- **Monumental stone**, cut so precisely that a knife will not go between the blocks. Terraces climbing whole mountainsides. Cities on ridges that no sane settler would build on.
- **Roads.** A network of paved roads that crosses the entire island, with way-stations at a day's walk, bridges that still stand, stairs cut into cliffs. The roads outlived everything else. The settlers use them daily and think about them rarely, the way people use a Roman road. **In the game, the road-node network is theirs**: every road the player walks was laid by the Builders, and every town sits where a road happened to be.
- **Machines.** Drones and mechs that still walk the ruins doing jobs nobody understands. Some patrol. Some repair things that are already broken. Some seem to wait.
- **No writing** that anybody can read. Knotted cords, tally marks, and carved images that might be a script and might be decoration.
- **Scale that almost fits.** Doorways a little too tall. Stairs a little too steep. Statues that look human from a distance and less so up close.

What they were called, they did not leave. Settlers say "the Builders", "the old people", "them". The highland people have their own name for them, which they do not translate.

### 2. The Silence

Whatever ended the Builders. Nobody knows how long it lasted. It could have been a war, a plague, a departure, a machine that was turned on and never turned off. The ruins carry no obvious wound: no burned cities, no mass graves that anyone has found. They look abandoned rather than destroyed. *See "What the game never says" below.*

### 3. The Arrival

Humans came a few generations ago, and their own account of it does not agree with itself. Every town has a founding story and the stories contradict each other:

- A ship, or many ships, wrecked on the western reefs.
- A door in the highlands that people walked out of and that closed behind them.
- Everyone simply waking on a beach with no memory of before.
- Descent from Builders who fell so far they forgot themselves.

The founding generation is dead. Their children are old. Nobody has a document from before the Arrival, or if somebody does, they are not showing it. There is no known home to go back to, and no serious person talks about leaving.

Whether other peoples arrived the same way, at other times, is for the peoples' design to say (01).

### 4. The Founding and now

The first towns were built on the coast, on Builder roads, out of timber, wind and steam. Trade between towns grew a guild. Towns that wanted protection grew militias, and the militias grew into something like a law. Between the towns, the roads filled with caravans, nomads and the people who rob them.

For three or four generations that was enough. The coast was wide, the interior was somebody else's, and nobody needed it.

Now the good coastal land is taken. Population has grown. The towns have begun to look uphill, where `[the highland people]` live among the ruins, and they have noticed. The first surveys have gone up the old roads. Some of them came back.

## The present balance

There is no single war. There are several powers who would fight each other given a reason, and the reasons are accumulating:

- **The coastal towns pushing uphill** against `[the highland people]`. This is the war the player sees first, the front line on the map, the one the militias are arming for.
- **The towns against each other.** The guild, the militias and the warlord-mayors do not agree on who runs the coast, and the uphill push is partly an excuse to arm.
- **Everyone courting the `[Technicians]`** (2026-09-15). The one institution that builds high tech sells to both sides of every quarrel and is the reason the uphill push has not happened yet: nobody wants to march without their machines, and the Technicians are in no hurry.
- **Everyone against the machines**, quietly. Nobody has time for it, and the machines do not seem to want anything, until a survey party goes somewhere it should not.
- **Outlaws against all of the above**, and against each other.

The player's actions tip this. Kenshi's shape: the world has a state, the state drifts, and a squad with enough force or enough money changes it.

## Starts

The game does not give the player a reason. It gives a start, which is a situation and a loadout:

- **Trader**: a cart, some coins, goods, a mule, and a guild letter. Debts.
- **Explorer**: a map with gaps in it, a mule, a pneumatic rifle and a reason to go uphill.
- **Homesteaders**: a family or a crew with tools, seed and no money, on unclaimed land.
- **Deserter**: one unit, a militia weapon, and people looking for it.
- **Outlaw band**, and a start for each of the other peoples once they are designed.

Starts are a menu of situations, not of stories. Each one is a set of items, a faction reputation table and a spawn region.

## What the game never says

These are the load-bearing mysteries. A data row, a faction blurb or an NPC line must not resolve them. Every in-game source that talks about them should disagree with some other in-game source.

1. **What the Builders were.** Human, not human, something that made humans. All three are believed by somebody.
2. **What ended them.** No account. Ruins look abandoned, not destroyed.
3. **How humans arrived.** Four founding stories, no evidence.
4. **Whether any people now on the island descends from the Builders.** Somebody will claim it. Nothing confirms it: no living people's tech obviously derives from Builder tech, and nobody's body obviously fits a Builder doorway.
5. **What the machines are for.** They act like they have a job. Nobody knows the job.
6. **Whether there is anything beyond the sea.** No ship has come from elsewhere in living memory. Fishing boats that go too far do not return, or return with nothing to report.

**Six, and only six.** Everything else about the past can be settled. Every protected mystery costs
every later piece of text, so the list does not grow without a reason.

**Disagreement is not noise.** Contradicting sources read as mystery only when each account is a
plausible distortion of the same real thing: repeated wrong, lied about, guessed from a fragment.
Three rumours invented independently read as an author who had not thought about it.

### A sealed answer, if one is ever wanted

The bible does not decide what actually happened, and does not need to for a long time. If a buried truth is ever wanted for a late-game reveal, it should be **one** truth, chosen then, and it must not contradict anything shipped. Candidates, none chosen:

- The Builders left on purpose, and everyone now on the island is a later arrival on a place that was deliberately emptied.
- The Builders are one of the peoples still here, who fell and rebuilt as something smaller and do not admit it.
- The island is a machine, and the drones are maintaining it.
- Humans were brought, and the "door" story is the true one.

## Open

- Whether a "far East" exists across the sea, and whether anything ever arrives from it. The bible currently says no ship has come in living memory, which leaves the door open.
- How long ago the Arrival was, in years. "A few generations" is deliberately vague until the subrace and town-age data need a number.
- Whether the Silence left a physical mark anywhere (a crater, a dead zone) or is entirely absent.
