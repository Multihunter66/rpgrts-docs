# The island

Draft 1, 2026-09-04. Geography, zones, resources, and what the first test level should contain.

## Shape

An island of roughly 100 km across (the standing world target), rising from a settled coast through foothills to a highland interior. The Builders lived high; the humans live low. The whole island is inhabited, and the interior is not a wilderness but somebody else's country.

Think of the Peruvian Andes compressed onto an island, and take the analogy literally: **the coast is desert.** Dry scrub, salt flats, dune and scoured rock, a river mouth every few days' walk where the only farmland is. Above it dry terraced highlands, a spine of peaks with ruins on the ridges, and snow only at the very top. The island is hostile by default (user decision 2026-09-04: "eher lebensfeindlich als grüne Idylle"); green is what people make with water, never what the land gives.

## Zones

Each zone is a gameplay region with its own peoples, resources and danger. None of them is empty, and none of them is only enemies.

### The coast and lowlands

The settlers' country, which is a desert shore with oases. Towns sit at river mouths and on Builder cisterns; between them are days of scrub, salt and dune along the old road. Fishing, the Guild's harbours, irrigated wheat in the river valleys, and homesteads pushing up every valley that has water. Timber is scarce and comes from the river woods, so a plank house is wealth and a palisade is an investment; towns are built as cast-stone bunkers instead, battered walls and flat roofs, Kenshi's shape (see the art bible). Planting a tree costs water that could grow wheat, so nobody plants one who does not have to. **Water is the resource**: a well is a claim, a cistern is a town. Roads busy with caravans. Militias. This is where every human start begins and where the player's first town is likely to be.

Threats: outlaws on the roads, town rivalries, Guild debt. Nothing here wants to kill you on sight except a bandit with a grudge.

### The foothills

The frontier. Where the coast is pushing up and `[the highland people]` are pushing back. Half-built settler towns, old highland sites recently abandoned or recently fortified, Builder way-stations occupied by whoever got there first. The best iron and stone. Prospectors, deserters, land-grabbers, highland scouts.

This is the front line of the visible war and the most volatile zone. A player who wants a fight, a fortune or a cause finds all three here.

### The highlands

The highland people's country (who they are is open, 01). Terraced valleys, stone towns, ruins that are still lived in, and the great Builder cities on the ridges that even they do not enter. Cold, thin air, hard going. Travel is on Builder roads and stairs; leaving them is slow and dangerous.

They have towns, markets, institutions and a law. A human who behaves can trade here. A human who arrives with a survey party and a militia escort cannot. Machines are common enough that everyone knows the rules for walking past them.

### The dead zones

Places inside the highlands and in a few spots lower down where the machines are dense, purposeful and hostile, and where the Builder ruins are richest. Not a region; a set of sites. Nobody lives in one. Salvagers go in, and the ones who come out carry laser cells, alloy plate and stories.

This is the "half the map is enemies" that the bible explicitly does not want, shrunk to the size where it is a destination instead of a wall.

### The sea

The one thing on the island that lives without being made to. The land is dead by default and the sea is not: fish, weed, birds. (If anyone asks where a treeless island gets its air, the answer is the same as on Earth, where most of it comes from the plankton in the sea and the atmosphere is a buffer far too large for one island's trees to matter. The question never reaches the player; it is answered here so nobody plants a forest to fix it.) Fishing near the coast. Nothing beyond it in living memory: boats that go too far do not return, or return with nothing. Whether there is a far shore is a sealed mystery. There are no harbours for anything larger than a fishing boat, because nothing larger has ever needed one.

## The roads

The Builder road network crosses every zone and is the island's circulatory system. It is paved, graded, bridged and stepped, and it does not care about the borders people have drawn since. A road runs from a Guild harbour straight up through the front line into a highland market town and on to a dead zone, because that is where the Builders wanted to go. Caravans, armies and refugees all use it, sometimes on the same day.

In the game the road nodes *are* this network. Roads that people built since are dirt tracks and do not get nodes.

## Resources by zone

Using the item names that already exist:

| Resource | Coast | Foothills | Highlands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood | River valleys only; scarce | Some, in the gullies | Scarce; the highlands build in stone |
| Stone | Some | Plentiful | Plentiful, and Builder-cut |
| Iron | Poor, near-surface | The good seams | Highland bronze instead |
| Wheat / food | Farms | Homesteads | Terraces; different crops later |
| Water | River mouths, wells; the limiting resource | Deep wells, springs | Cisterns, Builder aqueducts |
| Builder salvage | Rare finds | Way-stations | Dead zones |

The iron gradient is the economic engine of the frontier war: the coast has poor iron and wants the foothills' seams. The water gradient is the engine of everything else: nobody lives more than a day from water, and whoever holds a spring holds the valley.

## The first test level

The user's stated intent: *a test level with a couple of settlements and resources.* From the bible, the smallest level that exercises the world is a **foothill valley**:

- One **settler town** at the valley mouth, on a road node: mill, smithy, shop, militia, a generator if the power slice is to be tested.
- One **highland town** at the valley head, on the same road: stone, terraces, bronze, a market, patrols. Whose town it is waits on the peoples' design; the aesthetic does not.
- A **Builder way-station** halfway, empty or held by outlaws, with one machine that ignores you.
- **Resources** along the road: a wood stand near the settler town, a stone quarry and an iron seam in the middle, water at both ends.
- A **bandit camp** off the road.
- **Starts** at the settler town (trader, homesteaders) and on the road (explorer).

That is two settlements, one contested site, one road, every resource, and the front line, in one valley. It is small enough to stage with the existing tools and large enough that every faction seam gets used.
