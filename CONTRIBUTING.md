# Contributing

This page exists so you know what you are getting into before you spend an evening on something.
It is plain language, not a legal document.

## What this project is

A squad-based open-world RTS/RPG for PC, built in Unreal Engine 5.8. No studio, no publisher, no
budget, no deadlines, and no money involved at any point so far.

**The intention is to sell it.** There is no date and no guarantee it ever gets finished, but this
is not a free project that might one day drift into being commercial. Selling it is the plan.

You should decide whether to help knowing that, rather than finding it out later. That is the
whole reason this paragraph is the second thing on the page.

## Rights

**You keep the copyright on what you make.** It stays yours. Put it in your portfolio, show it
anywhere you like, reuse it in your own work.

**You give me permission to use it in this game**, including in a version that is sold, and
including after modification, since art and text almost always get adjusted to fit. That
permission does not expire, because a game cannot ship with a piece that might be withdrawn later.

**I will not sell your work on its own.** It goes into the game, not into an asset pack.

**If it ships and sells**, everyone whose work is in it gets a copy of the game and a credit.
That much I can promise flatly, and it is the floor rather than the ceiling.

**For anything substantial, talk to me before you start.** An afternoon on something small is one
thing. A week on a character model for a game that intends to charge money is another, and nobody
should do the second on the strength of a page on a website. I would rather sort out what is fair
beforehand, even if that turns out to be a credit and a copy of the game, than have it come up
after the work already exists.

## Credit

Everyone whose work is used is credited by the name they choose, in the game and in the repository.
If you would rather not be credited, say so.

## What you may not submit

**Anything you did not make or do not have the right to give.** This is the one rule with no
flexibility, because it is the thing that can genuinely kill the project rather than just annoy
someone.

Specifically, and this catches people out:

* Assets ripped or extracted from another game. Ever, for any reason, including as a placeholder.
* Content from paid asset packs. Owning a licence to use something yourself almost never includes
  the right to hand it to someone else.
* Models, textures, music or text you found online without a licence that clearly permits this.
  "It was free to download" is not a licence.
* Writing copied from another game's world, a novel, or a wiki.

Public domain and CC0 material is fine. Anything under a licence with conditions, such as CC-BY
requiring attribution, is usually fine too, but tell me which licence it is so the requirement can
actually be met.

**AI-generated work is allowed.** Most of the current placeholder art in this game was generated,
so it would be strange to forbid it. Say which tool you used, because some services claim rights
over their output and that has to be checked once rather than discovered later.

## How decisions get made

I read everything, I decide, and I say why. Not everything can make it in. Usually that is a
question of what the game can already do, not of whether the idea is good. When something does
land, it goes into the docs and you are credited.

Most refusals come down to the same thing: the idea needs machinery the game does not have. That
is worth knowing before you start, which is why the design pages exist and why they say what the
systems will and will not support.

## Before you start something big

Say what you are planning first, in a thread. Not for permission, but because five minutes of
"that will not fit, here is why" beats a weekend of work that cannot be used. For anything small,
just make it and show it.

## How to submit

Post it in the Discord. Files, images, text, whatever form it takes. There is no pipeline to learn
and no repository access needed. I handle the import.

For 3D work, check whether there is an authoring spec for that kind of asset first. The character
body has one, and it is exact about scale, skeleton and export settings, because those are the
things that make an otherwise good asset unusable.
