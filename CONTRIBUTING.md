# Contributing

Plain language, not a legal document.

## What this project is

A squad-based open-world RTS/RPG for PC, built in Unreal Engine 5.8. No studio, no publisher, no
budget, no deadlines, and no money involved at any point so far.

**The intention is to sell it.** No date, no guarantee it gets finished, but selling it is the
plan. Decide whether to help knowing that.

## Rights

**You keep the copyright on what you make.** It stays yours. Put it in your portfolio, show it
anywhere you like, reuse it in your own work.

**You give me permission to use it in this game**, including a sold version, including after
modification. The permission does not expire; a game cannot ship with a piece that might be
withdrawn.

**I will not sell your work on its own.** It goes into the game, not into an asset pack.

**If it ships and sells**, everyone whose work is in it gets a copy and a credit. That is the
floor.

**For anything substantial, talk to me before you start.** A week on a character model for a
game that intends to charge money should not happen on the strength of a web page. Sort out what
is fair beforehand.

## Credit

Everyone whose work is used is credited by the name they choose, in the game and in the repository.
If you would rather not be credited, say so.

## What you may not submit

**Anything you did not make or do not have the right to give.** The one rule with no
flexibility; it can kill the project.

Specifically:

* Assets ripped or extracted from another game. Ever, for any reason, including as a placeholder.
* Content from paid asset packs. Owning a licence to use something yourself almost never includes
  the right to hand it to someone else.
* Models, textures, music or text you found online without a licence that clearly permits this.
  "It was free to download" is not a licence.
* Writing copied from another game's world, a novel, or a wiki.

Public domain and CC0 material is fine. Anything under a licence with conditions, such as CC-BY
requiring attribution, is usually fine too, but tell me which licence it is so the requirement can
actually be met.

**AI-generated work is allowed.** Most of the placeholder art was generated. Say which tool,
because some services claim rights over their output.

## How decisions get made

I read everything, I decide, and I say why. Most refusals are the same thing: the idea needs
machinery the game does not have, which is why the design pages say what the systems will and will
not support. When something lands, it goes into the docs and you are credited.

## Before you start something big

Say what you are planning first, in a thread. Five minutes of "that will not fit" beats a
weekend of work that cannot be used. For anything small, just make it and show it.

## How to submit

Post it in the Discord, in whatever form. No pipeline, no repository access; I handle the import.

For 3D work, check for an authoring spec first. The character body has one, exact about scale,
skeleton and export settings.
